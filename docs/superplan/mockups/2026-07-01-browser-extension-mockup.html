The Science of Memory

Learning new vocabulary depends on context and repetition. When you read a word in a meaningful sentence, your brain builds stronger connections than from flashcards alone.

Take the word charge, for instance. It can mean an electrical charge or a legal accusation. If you encounter it while reading a story, the memory lasts longer.

Idioms like spaced repetition or kick the bucket are also easier to remember when attached to a personal story.
